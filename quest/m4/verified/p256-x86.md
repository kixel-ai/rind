# [L] P-256 ECDH and ECDSA are proven on x86_64

## Goal

P-256 key agreement, ECDSA signing, and ECDSA verification, which every TLS certificate chain with an ECDSA key exercises, earn the ✓ on x86_64, as they do on aarch64.

## Plan

- Already proven on x86_64: P-256 agreement, `p256_agree` in `Rind/X86_64/P256Agree.lean`, which `admin/prove` also writes for aarch64, down to s2n-bignum's `p256_scalarmul` and its `_alt` form, which d·Q runs on both targets. Its stored-key ECDH measured as fast as the `p256_montjscalarmul` it replaced (minimum 24.9-25.1 µs against 25.0 µs), so there is no conversion to affine left to prove. On aarch64, all the shared Rust: key generation in `Rind/Aarch64/P256.lean` and ECDSA in `Rind/Aarch64/P256Ecdsa.lean` and `P256Signature.lean`, whose lemmas already take x86_64's `Runs`.
- Remaining runs, all for k·G (`base_multiply`, opaque in x86_64's translation): aws-lc's `ecp_nistz256_select_w7` (SSE2) or `ecp_nistz256_avx2_select_w7`, and `ecp_nistz256_point_add_affine` or its BMI2 and ADX form `_affinex` with their field helpers, under rind's Booth-7 driver over a 152 KB table of 37 rows of 64 affine points (`mid/p256.rs`, `low/x86_64/ecp_nistz256.rs`). Verification's u1·G reads the same table.
- s2n-bignum's routines are no faster route for k·G: its proven x86_64 `p256_scalarmulbase` cost 12-17% ([moq-dev/rind#234](https://github.com/moq-dev/rind/pull/234)), and its `p256_montjmixadd` under rind's driver cost key generation 13% (a 6-bit table, signing 8%, or a 7-bit one). Rind's Rust select with aws-lc's addition cost key generation 2.4% and signing nothing (-0.5%); see Waiting.
- Route:
  1. HOL Light proofs of aws-lc's mixed addition, and of its select unless the Waiting decision replaces it, in s2n-bignum's x86_64 model against its P-256 specs. The model lacks instructions they use, among them PCMPEQD, VPCMPEQD, VPERMD, VMOVD, and PREFETCHT0; extend `proofs/hol-light/s2n-bignum/x86.patch`, which [moq-dev/rind#627](https://github.com/moq-dev/rind/pull/627) started with POR.
  2. Aeneas for the Booth driver, and a proof that the x86_64 table holds the multiples of G those proofs assume, as `Rind/Aarch64/P256Table.lean` does for aarch64's.
  3. Move key generation and ECDSA's proofs from `Rind/Aarch64/` into `Rind/X86_64/`, for `admin/prove` to copy back, as agreement's are, with the x86_64 axioms ECDSA adds to `Rind/X86_64/S2nBignum.lean` (`p256_montjadd`, `p256_montjdouble`, `p256_montjmixadd`, `bignum_inv_p256`, `bignum_neg_p256`, `bignum_mod_n256`, and the ADX routines' `_alt` forms), and add the x86_64 rows to `benchmarks/verified.toml`.
- Upstream: offering this work to awslabs/s2n-bignum is optional, and there is no standing approval: stop and ask the maintainer before posting there or anywhere outside the moq-dev and kixelated orgs (decided with the maintainer on 2026-11-07 on [moq-dev/rind#448](https://github.com/moq-dev/rind/pull/448)).

## Waiting

- Maintainer: whether k·G keeps aws-lc's select. Options: (a) run rind's Rust select, `bignum_point_select`'s `select_avx2::<8, 2>` or `select_sse2::<8, 4>`, which P-384 and P-521 share and which Lean can prove through intrinsic models like `Rind/X86_64/Simd.lean`'s, as [P-384's quest](/quest/m4/verified/p384.md) plans, at 2.4% on key generation and nothing on signing; (b) keep aws-lc's `select_w7` and `avx2_select_w7` and prove them in HOL Light, after adding PCMPEQD, VPCMPEQD, VPERMD, VMOVD, and PREFETCHT0 to s2n-bignum's model. Recommend (a): one select proof serves three curves, and the model extensions shrink to those aws-lc's addition needs. An agent cannot take it: AGENTS.md asks for the fastest source, and (a) trades 2.4% of key generation for a smaller trust base, which is the maintainer's call. Check: this item is replaced by the choice in Plan.
