# [XL] AES-GCM on x86_64 with VAES is proven

## Goal

AES-GCM earns the ✓ on x86_64 CPUs with VAES and VPCLMULQDQ (Ice Lake and later, Zen 3 and later), the common tier on today's x86_64 servers and desktops: BoringSSL's VAES kernels, and rind's choice among them, Vale's, and its own, are proven.

## Plan

- Runs (`low/x86_64/aes_gcm_vaes.rs`): BoringSSL's `aes_gcm_{enc,dec}_update_vaes_avx2` from 256 bytes, and `aes_gcm_{enc,dec}_update_vaes_avx512` from 2 KB where the CPU has AVX-512, with their key-power setup `gcm_init_vpclmulqdq_avx2` and `_avx512` in BoringSSL's table layout; below those lengths, rind's by-8 and AVX-512 by-16 intrinsics (proven, given this tier's key powers), and Vale's proven routines for opens under 256 bytes on cores without AVX-512.
- Already proven: Vale's routines, the AEAD API's glue on Vale's tier, and of BoringSSL's only the two key setups. No published proof of BoringSSL's VAES or AVX-512 kernels exists, and Vale has none. pyca/verified-garbage proves its own AVX-512 AES-GCM, which rind runs on Zen 5 from 6 KB, but rind imports none of its theorems yet.
- Done: `gcm_init_vpclmulqdq_avx2` is proven in Lean on VG's x86_64 model (`proofs/Rind/X86_64/GcmInitAvx2.lean`, `gcm_init_vpclmulqdq_avx2_correct`): for the key in the two words at `rsi`, high first, the table's first eight blocks are H^8 down to H^1, each shifted as Vale's `shift_key_1` does (`GhashClmul.lean`'s `TableFor` form), and its next 64 bytes their halves XORed in BoringSSL's order, with its frame, ABI, and `ConstantTime`. `RindIsa/X86_64.lean` models the RIP-relative static reads (`rip`) and `vpshufd` from memory it needs, `admin/prove lean` checks x86_64 functions' relocations against their statics, and `Cosim/RindX86_64.lean` cosimulates `rip`.
- Done: `gcm_init_vpclmulqdq_avx512` is proven the same way (`proofs/Rind/X86_64/GcmInitAvx512.lean`, `gcm_init_vpclmulqdq_avx512_correct`): the table's sixteen blocks are H^16 down to H^1, each shifted, which is `Avx512Powers`' form of `VaesKernel::powers`, with its frame (it changes `rax` and `r8`), ABI, and `ConstantTime`, over the EVEX forms `RindIsa/X86_64.lean` adds for it (`vpternlogd` and `vbroadcasti32x4` of a static, `vpxord` of `ymm` registers, `vshufi64x2`, `vmovdqu8` to memory) and a 32-bit `dec`, which `admin/prove vg` cosimulates under SDE on a CPU without AVX-512. Nothing restates either key setup's theorem in the Rust proofs yet: an axiom about each naked function, as `Rind/X86_64/RsaMont.lean` restates `bn_mul4x_mont_correct`, and a model of `VaesKernel::new` and `powers` in place of `AesGcmModel.lean`'s opaque ones, would discharge `Avx512Powers` and, through `ClmulTable::from_powers`, `TableFor` on both tiers.
- The update kernels' text does not lay out as `Code`'s `ite` and `loop`, which `layout` and so `admin/prove lean`'s bytes check need: each tail jumps into the middle of another path. The AVX2 kernels' `jz .Lvaes_avx2_reduce` skips `.Lvaes_avx2_lessthan64bytes`, which their `jb` also targets, and their one-to-three-block dispatch ends two paths with a `jmp` to the GHASH multiply the third falls into; the AVX-512 kernels' `jz .Lvaes_avx512_reduce` skips `.Lvaes_avx512_partial_vec`, which their `jb` also targets. Their main loops, the AES-128/192/256 entry, and the round loops do lay out. So proving them needs a decision (see Waiting): rind's structured copies, as `admin/chacha20poly1305-x86/generate` writes aws-lc's AVX2 seal, re-testing a flag where the text shares a tail, is the precedent.
- Route: Lean proofs of BoringSSL's four kernels and two key setups on VG's x86_64 model, with their leakage contracts, as [kixel-ai/rind#912](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/912) proved aarch64 SHA-256. VG's model already has EVEX `vaesenc`, `vaesenclast`, and `vpclmulqdq`; model what the kernels use beyond it, such as the VEX.256 forms, in `proofs/verified-garbage/RindIsa/`. List each program in `RindIsa/Proven.lean`, so `admin/prove lean` checks its bytes, relocations, and statics and `admin/prove vg` cosimulates it, as `gcm_init_vpclmulqdq_avx2`'s are. The kernels' other statics (`AVX2_BSWAP_MASK`, `AVX2_CTR_PATTERN`, `AVX2_INC_2BLOCKS`, and the AVX-512 ones) read through `rip`, adding `RipOp` forms as the kernels need them.
- The spec: `common/gcm.ml` and `common/fips197.ml` at rind's s2n-bignum pin, `3f01260f`, which has [awslabs/s2n-bignum#490](https://github.com/awslabs/s2n-bignum/pull/490)'s rewrite of both and their known-answer tests (`common/gcm_kats.ml`, `common/fips197_kats.ml`).
- Then Aeneas to Lean for rind's glue around the kernels and the length-based choice, as `Rind/X86_64/AesGcm.lean` does for Vale's tier.
- The two GCM specs (maintainer, 2026-11-09): ✅ prove them equal in Lean (rejected: amend the composition rule to allow a spec-equivalence axiom). Done, with no axiom beyond Lean's own: `Rind/S2nGcm.lean` ([kixel-ai/rind#1045](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/1045)) proves `aes128_cipher` and `aes256_cipher` Vale's `eval_cipher`, the kernels' counter blocks and keystream Vale's, and `nist_ghash` Vale's GHASH, and `Rind/S2nGcmAe.lean` proves `common/gcm.ml`'s `gctr` Vale's from any counter block (`gctr_eq`), `gcm_ae` and `gcm_ad` with a 96-bit IV and whole blocks `gcm_encrypt_LE` and `gcm_decrypt_LE` for any cipher that is Vale's AES under the key (`gcmAe_eq`, `gcmAd_eq`), and `common/fips197.ml`'s AES-128 and AES-256 key schedules Vale's (`aes128KeyExpansion_eq`, `aes256KeyExpansion_eq`, and `gcmAe_aes128` to `gcmAd_aes256` end to end).
  - s2n-bignum's spec has no AES-192 (`common/fips197.ml` defines `aes128_cipher` and `aes256_cipher` only), but BoringSSL's VAES kernels take AES-192 keys. `gcmAe_eq` and `gcmAd_eq` take any cipher that is Vale's AES, so an AES-192 kernel theorem over `gcm_ae` needs only an `aes192_cipher` and its key schedule beside them; stating that kernel's theorem over Vale's `gctr_encrypt_LE` and a GHASH from the supplied counter block and accumulator (`ghashPoly` seeded with the incoming accumulator: both dispatch arms hash the AAD before the kernel, so it can be nonzero, which `ghash_LE` from zero cannot describe), then composing those into `gcm_encrypt_LE`, needs neither. A kernel call is an update step from a state, not a whole message from the IV, so `gcm_encrypt_LE` alone does not express it.
  - The kernels are update steps, GCTR from a counter block and GHASH from an accumulator, which `gctr_eq` and `nistGhash_eq` state from any. `gctr` takes whole blocks: so does the AVX2 kernel, but the AVX-512 one masks a partial last block (`low/x86_64/aes_gcm_vaes.rs:90`), so its theorem needs a spec past `gctr`'s, such as Vale's `gctr_encrypt_LE` directly.
- The AVX-512 tier below 2 KB is proven over two predicates this quest discharges from `gcm_init_vpclmulqdq_avx512_correct`: `Avx512Powers H t`, that `VaesKernel::powers` gives the hash key's 16 descending powers, and `TableFor H t.clmul`, that the PCLMULQDQ table `from_powers` builds holds the first eight. Given them, `tagged_from_avx512_spec` (`proofs/Rind/X86_64/AesGcmAvx512.lean`) is `tagged_from_spec`'s ciphertext and tag under 2 KB, by-16 from 256 bytes and by-8 below, so the tier's seals and opens compose it with the kernel's proof from 2 KB, as `Rind/X86_64/AesGcmClmul.lean` composes `tagged_from_spec`.
- [kixel-ai/rind#955](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/955) measured pyca/verified-garbage's AVX-512 kernels under rind's glue and kept BoringSSL's, so AVX-512 CPUs' long messages stay this quest's to prove; but Zen 5 runs VG's from 6 KB ([kixel-ai/rind#1139](https://github.com/kixel-ai/rind/pull/1139)), so there restate VG's `sealPreparedContract` and `openPreparedContract` as axioms, as `Rind/X86_64/VgChaCha20Poly1305.lean` does its routines', and drop the three routines from `admin/vg-binding`'s `UNCOVERED`.
- Do the AVX2 kernel first: it is the whole path on Zen 3 and on cores without AVX-512.

## Waiting

- Maintainer: BoringSSL's four AES-GCM update kernels cannot be proven as their text stands, because each tail jumps into the middle of another path, which rind's Lean model of x86_64 cannot lay out as structured code, so its bytes check cannot bind a proof to them. How should they be proven?
  - Structured copies (Recommended) - a generator beside `admin/import-boringssl`, as `admin/chacha20poly1305-x86/generate` does for aws-lc's AVX2 seal, writes rind's copy of each kernel with BoringSSL's instructions but a re-tested flag or an extra jump where the text shares a tail, and its Lean program; costs a branch on the tail and a benchmark against BoringSSL's, listed in ALGORITHMS.md as a deliberate change
  - Whole chunks only - keep BoringSSL's bytes, model the tails' jumps as instructions the model faults on, and have the Rust pass the kernels only whole 128- or 512-byte chunks, finishing the rest with the proven by-8 and by-16 intrinsics; slower on lengths that are not whole chunks, and adds a construct to the trusted model
  - Unstructured code in the model - add labels and jumps to rind's extension of VG's model, with their own semantics and constant-time notion beside VG's `Code`; the largest growth of the trusted base
